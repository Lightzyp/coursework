Lab Purpose (In Your Own Words)

The purpose of this lab was to evaluate the physical security controls of campus building and identity potential vulnerabilities. Instead of focusing on digital threats, the label emphasized how physical weaknesses can impact overall security. The goal was to observe public areas, access or physical security breaches.

Tools & Resources Required

On-site observation of a main campus building

Visual inspection of entrances, exits, and public areas

Course lab instructions and guidelines

Notes and documentation of findings

Lab Procedures

Selected a main campus academic building at East Tennessee State University to conduct my physical security audit. I remained in public areas only and didn’t attempt to access restricted or private sections of the building. Then I Began my assessment outside the building NY observing 

exterior lighting

Visible security cameras

Entrance signage

Emergency exits

Landscaping and nearby object (Benches, dumpsters, tress)

I examined the main entrance to determine what type of access control was in place. I observed whether doors required key cards, whether security guards were present, and how effective the entry system appeared. I evaluated whether visitors were monitored upon entry or if individuals could enter freely without verification. I also assessed whether sensitive materials appeared unsecured and whether trash disposal areas could potentially expose confidential documents. Finally, I documented vulnerabilities and developed recommendations for improving physical security controls.

Observations

During the audit, I observed that the main entrance doors were unlocked during business hours and did not require key card access for entry. While this is common for academic buildings, it allows anyone to enter freely without identity verification. I did notice visible security cameras near entrances, which may serve as a deterrent. However, some hallways appeared to have limited camera coverage, potentially creating blind spots.Interior classroom doors were generally unlocked when not in use, and some offices were secured while others were not. Visitors were not actively monitored and could move throughout public areas without restriction. I also noticed that trash receptacles were accessible and not secured, which could potentially expose discarded documents. Exterior lighting was adequate near the main entrance but less consistent near side entrances. Emergency exits were clearly marked and accessible, which is positive from a safety perspective. Overall, while basic physical security controls were in place, there were areas where improvements could strengthen protection against unauthorized access.
