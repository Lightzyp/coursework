What FERPA COVERS: Protected Data Types

The protected data types are (20 U.S.C. 1232g), known as the Family educational rights and privacy act (FERPA) of 1974 is a federal law protecting the privacy of student’s education records. This also applies to schools receiving U.S Department of education funds, granting parents and students over 18 in college the right to inspect records and seek amendment and control disclosures. I should believe that ETSU has this in place and my parents ran into a wall when trying to ask about my grade, which obviously gave them full transparency when it comes to my education.  This is a broad definition that IT must take seriously as it extends beyond paper transcripts. Based on a reading of the statue, the type of information that falls under FERPA protection includes:

Grades, transcript, and academic performance records

Enrollment records and call schedules

Financial aid records and billing information 

Disciplinary records

Personally identifiable information (PII) such ass Student ID number, Social Security Numbers, Date of Birth, and contact information 

What groups are affected by the Law

Groups that are affected by the law are any schools that receive federal funding. This really includes every public university in the country and most private ones as well under 20 U.S.C. §1232g(a)(1). The key groups are students, parents/guardians, eligible students, school officials, and third-party contractors. The institution itself and ultimately its leadership bears legal responsibility. Federal funding can be withheld by any institution with a policy or practice of unlawfully releasing education records per under 20 U.S.C. §1232g(a)(1). That is what happened to Big Brain University, and it is the risk that GlobalED must actively work on to Eliminate.

Recommendation

FERPA requires that access be limited to individuals with a legitimate educational interest (34 CFR §99.31). Role-based Access Control ensures employees only access the data necessary for their jo functions.

Impact on Organization:

Reduces risk of unauthorized data access

Improves accountability and auditing 

May slow down workflows if access approval processes become more strict 

Cost of enforcement:

Add access management software

IT staff can configure permissions

Training employees in new access procedures 

Encryption of Student Records:

Encryption should apply to:

Student Records within the school’s database

File storage system

Encryption if student information is being sent across the network

Stronger protection of sensitive records

Possible minor system performance overhead
