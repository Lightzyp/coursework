Chapter 14 “Happy” Reflection

After watching Happy, I felt both inspired and grounded by the reminder that happiness doesn’t come from fame or fortune -it’s rooted in connection purpose and compassion. The film showcased people from different countries. Cultures and walks of life who found joy in relationships, community support, and meaningful activity rather than material possessions. I was especially touched by the story of the man from India w despite his low-income rickshaw job, found happiness though his relationships and sense of gratitude.

One of my favorite facts from the documentary was that 40% of our happiness is within our control, based on our intentional actions and mindset. This stood out to me because it suggests we have real power to improve our wellbeing through habits like gratitude, kindness, and nurturing relationship. My favorite part of the film was the section on flow- how people feel their happiest when they’re fully engaged in something they love. It made me think about the moments I lose track of time doing something I enjoy and how those experiences really do feel the most fulfilling

 
Chapter 15  Psychological Disorder in Media

One example of psychological disorder in media is the Character Monk from the TV show Monk. He has obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD). The show depicts Monk as having intense obsession with germs, order and cleanliness, which lead him to perform repetitive compulsions such ss cleaning, checking, and organizing. While the Show exaggerates some symptoms for comedic effect, it still captures many realistic aspects of OCD. I think the portrayal is somewhat accurate in showing how disruptive and time-consuming the disorder can be although it sometimes overlooks the emotional distress involved
